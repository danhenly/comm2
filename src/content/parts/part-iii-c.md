## 1. Due Process: Alternative Penalty, NTC Against Legislative Franchise, Hearing Rule

> "No franchise, certificate, or any other form of authorization for the operation of a public utility shall be granted except to citizens of the Philippines or to corporations or associations organized under the laws of the Philippines, at least sixty per centum of whose capital is owned by such citizens; nor shall such franchise, certificate, or authorization be exclusive in character or for a longer period than fifty years. Neither shall any such franchise or right be granted except under the condition that it shall be subject to amendment, alteration, or repeal by the Congress when the common good so requires. The State shall encourage equity participation in public utilities by the general public. The participation of foreign investors in the governing body of any public utility enterprise shall be limited to their proportionate share in its capital, and all the executive and managing officers of such corporation or association must be citizens of the Philippines."

> "To suspend or revoke any certificate issued under the provisions of this Act whenever the holder thereof has violated or willfully and contumaciously refused to comply with any order rule or regulation of the Commission or any provision of this Act: Provided, That the Commission, for good cause, may prior to the hearing suspend for a period not to exceed thirty days any certificate or the exercise of any right or authority issued or granted under this Act by order of the Commission, whenever such step shall in the judgment of the Commission be necessary to avoid serious and irreparable damage or inconvenience to the public or to private interests."

> "To amend, modify or revoke at any time certificate issued under the provisions of this Act, whenever the facts and circumstances on the strength of which said certificate was issued have been misrepresented or materially changed."

| Element or Requisite | Content |
|---|---|
| The ex parte rule | The regulator never revokes a certificate ex parte without previous notice and opportunity to explain, and every such revocation violates due process, Sec. 16(n), and settled doctrine |
| The alternative penalty | The Commission may impose a fine as one remedy among others since the last sentence of Sec. 21 makes the fine cumulative and additional, and the Court never substitutes its discretion where evidence supports the choice |
| Revocation over fine | Long violation of certificate conditions with incapability shown by inadequate service warrants outright revocation, because continued operation would sacrifice public interest to private interest |
| Revocation denied | Mere temporary non-operation from causes beyond control never warrants cancellation, and a single criminal misuse of a unit without operator complicity never cancels the innocent certificate |
| NTC against legislative franchise | The NTC has no power to cancel the legislative franchise behind a broadcast certificate, because cancellation of the operating license is a death sentence on speech that must survive strict scrutiny for a compelling state interest with the least restrictive means |
| Hearing defect cured | Absence of prior notice or hearing before suspension or revocation is cured by a full hearing on motion for reconsideration that raises every merit and defense the operator would have raised |
| Sec. 17 no-hearing powers | Investigation, compliance orders, uncertified-operation bans, expense assessments, bookkeeping and accounting orders, valuation, meter testing, special trip permits, accident inquiry, and law-compliance compulsion all proceed without previous hearing |
| Sec. 17 against Sec. 21 | Sec. 17 confers no power to fine while Sec. 21 confers it only after due notice and hearing, so every fine without hearing fails even when the investigation behind it was lawful |

A fine punishes the breach while revocation ends the privilege, so the two never exclude each other. A legislative franchise comes from Congress while a certificate comes from the regulator, so cancellation of one never cancels the other. A Sec. 17 investigation needs no prior hearing while a Sec. 21 fine always does, so the power invoked decides the process due.

### Case: *Rizal Light v. Morong*, G.R. Nos. L-20993 and L-21221, September 28, 1968

Facts: Rizal Light held a 1949 certificate for electric service in Morong, ignored voltage and metering directives from 1954, missed hearings and filings, and lost its certificate with forfeiture of its franchise, while Morong Electric took a municipal franchise before incorporation, incorporated a month later, and won its own certificate on findings of need and financial capacity.

Issue: The issue was whether long neglect of certificate conditions justified revocation instead of a fine and whether the later franchise survived the want of corporate existence at the grant.

Held: The Court held that both orders stood, because inspection reports substantially supported the findings, contumacious refusal since 1954 warranted revocation over a mere fine, protection of investment shields only operators of good standing, the later incorporation with acceptance perfected the municipal contract, and a municipal franchise takes effect only upon Commission approval.

Doctrine: The Sec. 21 fine is one cumulative remedy that never bars revocation. Willful and contumacious breach draws cancellation. The protection rule never rewards neglect. A hearing defect on reconsideration is cured where the operator litigated every merit and defense.

### Case: *Albano v. Reyes*, G.R. No. 83551, July 11, 1989

Facts: The PPA bid out the development, management, and operation of the Manila International Container Terminal, awarded the contract to ICTSI, and won presidential approval with directives keeping port planning and revenues with the PPA, while a taxpayer suit claimed that operation of the terminal as a public utility needed a congressional franchise under Art. XII Sec. 11.

Issue: The issue was whether a private operator of the container terminal needed a franchise specially granted by Congress before operating under the PPA contract with presidential approval.

Held: The Court held that no congressional franchise was necessary, because P.D. No. 857 lets the PPA operate by itself, by contract, or otherwise, E.O. No. 30 tasked the PPA with the port complex under that charter, and the PPA contract with presidential approval constituted the necessary authorization with a presumption of regularity.

Doctrine: Congress may delegate licensing so an agency contract with presidential approval needs no parallel franchise. Art. XII Sec. 11 lets Congress amend or repeal grants but never says only Congress may authorize operation. Courts leave bidder qualification to the agency absent grave abuse.

**Q: A certificate holder disobeys voltage and metering directives for eight straight years, cures nothing after inspection reports and hearings, and pleads that the Commission must impose only the daily fine. Must the Commission settle for a fine.**

A: No, because willful and contumacious refusal to obey orders and certificate conditions warrants revocation and the fine remedy is only cumulative and additional.
The law empowers the Commission to suspend or revoke where the holder violated or willfully and contumaciously refused to obey any order, rule, regulation, or provision of law, and the last sentence of Sec. 21 keeps every other remedy intact so a fine never blocks cancellation.
The ruling in Rizal Light v. Morong (G.R. Nos. L-20993 and L-21221, 1968) sustained revocation on exactly such an eight-year record of neglect since protection of investment shields only operators of good standing.
The Commission therefore revokes the certificate and forfeits the franchise on substantial evidence instead of stopping at a fine.

**Q: The NTC cancels the CPC of a broadcast station that operates under a congressional franchise, and the station invokes free speech and prior restraint. May the NTC end the franchise by cancelling the certificate.**

A: No, because the NTC has no power to cancel the legislative franchise behind the certificate and cancellation of the operating license operates as a death sentence on broadcast speech.
The Constitution protects broadcast media under the free-speech, expression, and press guarantees even while scarcity justifies regulation, and the franchise-plus-certificate framework already imposes fees, fines, and mandates through valid rule-making without ending the grant.
The ruling in Albano v. Reyes (G.R. No. 83551, 1989) confirms that franchise authority and agency authorization are distinct layers since a delegated contract with presidential approval can authorize operation where Congress supplied the delegation.
The NTC therefore keeps its fining and rule-making powers but leaves repeal of the franchise to Congress when the common good so requires.

**Q: The Commission suspended a certificate without prior hearing, the operator moved for reconsideration raising every merit and defense it would have raised at the original hearing, and the Commission granted partial relief after full argument. Was due process denied.**

A: No, because the later full hearing on reconsideration cured the earlier want of prior notice and hearing.
The law requires notice and hearing before suspension or revocation, yet the defect is procedural and curable where the operator actually adduced evidence, faced the record, and argued through counsel on reconsideration.
The ruling in Rizal Light v. Morong (G.R. Nos. L-20993 and L-21221, 1968) treats hearing objections as waived unless timely raised and cures process through the granted reconsideration.
The suspension therefore stands as a valid exercise of the Sec. 16(n) power with the thirty-day pre-hearing limit observed.

## 2. Rate Regulation: Police Power, Non-Delegation, Deregulation, Just Standard, Expenses, Provisional Increase, Collection

> "To fix and determine the fair and reasonable individual or joint rates, tolls, charges, classifications, tariffs or schedules thereof, as well as commutation, mileage, kilometrage, and other special rates which shall be imposed, observed, and followed thereafter by any public service when the public interest so requires: Provided, That the Commission may, in its discretion, approve rates proposed by public services provisionally and without necessity of any hearing; but it shall call a hearing thereon within fifteen (15) days, thereafter, upon publication and notice to the affected parties in the territory affected, to ratify its prior provisional approval or change, modify or later the approved rate based on public interest: Provided, further, That in case the public service equipment of an operator is used principally or secondarily for the promotion of a private business, the net profits of said private business shall be considered in relation with the public service of such operator for the purpose of fixing the rates: Provided, finally, That when the public interest requires, the Administrative Agency may establish and enforce a methodology for setting rates, taking into account all relevant considerations, including the efficiency of the regulated public service. The rates must be such as to allow the recovery of prudent and efficient costs and a reasonable rate of return to enable the public service to operate viably and efficiently. The Administrative Agency may adopt alternative forms consistent with internationally accepted rate-setting methodology. The adopted rate-setting methodology shall ensure a reasonable price of the commodity or service and that the rates prescribed shall not be discriminatory."

> "To adopt, establish, fix, impose, maintain, or collect or carry into effect any individual or joint rates, commutation, mileage or other special rate, toll, fare, charge, classification or itinerary. The Commission shall approve only those that are prudent and efficient and not any that are unjustly discriminatory or unduly preferential, only upon reasonable notice to the public services and other parties concerned, giving them a reasonable opportunity to be heard, and the burden of the proof to show that the proposed rates or regulation are prudent and efficient shall be upon the public service proposing the same."

| Element or Requisite | Content |
|---|---|
| Police-power basis | The State fixes utility rates under police power since private property devoted to public use and affected with public interest ceases to be purely private, and the regulation promotes the common good |
| The only standard | Every rate must be fair and reasonable, which balances a fair return on the property performing the service against a reasonable charge to the public, and no utility holds a vested right to any single valuation formula |
| Confiscatory against oppressive | A rate so low as to deny a reasonable return is confiscatory while a rate so high as to burden consumers is oppressive, and reasonableness is a question of fact calling for discretion, good sense, and independent judgment |
| Presumption and burden | Rates fixed by the agency are presumed reasonable, the challenger proves confiscation or oppression, and courts refuse to interfere absent abuse of discretion |
| Non-delegation | The legislature delegates rate fixing to the regulator, the regulator never subdelegates it to the operator, and every fare range letting carriers raise fares at will is an invalid undue delegation |
| Deregulation carve-outs | Sec. 16(c) never mandates rate regulation nor repeals the telecom, oil, power, domestic shipping, and BOT deregulation laws with their implementing issuances, and domestic shipping rates stand deregulated under R.A. No. 9295 |
| Non-discrimination | The methodology must yield a reasonable price with no discriminatory rates, carriers charge the same for like contemporaneous service under similar conditions, and no undue preference or prejudice survives |
| Private-business profits | Where utility equipment also promotes a private business, the net profits of that business enter the rate computation |
| Operating expenses | Only reasonable, recurring expenses attributable to production of the service enter the rate, so the utility recoups prudent costs over depreciation and earns a return above them |
| Income tax excluded | Income tax is an excise on the privilege of earning and benefits only the taxpayer, so it never enters operating expenses and never shifts to consumers |
| Depreciation allowance | The agency fixes proper and adequate depreciation rates, the utility carries the fund from earnings, and the fund spends only on depreciation, improvements, construction, extensions, or additions |
| Provisional increase | Urgent public need lets the agency approve rates provisionally without hearing since provisional rates are temporary and adjustable to the definitive rates after final hearing, with a mandatory hearing within fifteen days on publication and notice |
| Collection and refund | The utility collects only approved rates, overcollection draws refund in lieu of or in addition to the fine, even a once-approved rate falls where it offends the Constitution or statutes, and PUV and metering duties enforce exact charging |

Rate regulation protects the public against arbitrary and excessive rates while keeping service efficient and viable. The legislature fixes the policy while the agency applies it, so courts review only reasonableness as a judicial question. A provisional grant answers urgency on first look while only the final rate answers merit after hearing.

### Case: *Pangasinan Transportation v. PSC*, G.R. No. 47065, June 26, 1940

Facts: A bus operator challenged the Public Service Act and its rate and certificate regime as an invalid delegation and a taking, while the Commission defended regulation of fares and service as police power over a business affected with public interest.

Issue: The issue was whether the Public Service Act with its rate-fixing and certificate powers validly regulates operators as police power or unconstitutionally delegates and takes property.

Held: The Court held that the Act is constitutional, because public interest supplies a sufficient standard, a certificate is a mere privilege reachable by regulation, and new conditions on existing certificates need only notice and hearing to bind.

Doctrine: Rate regulation is valid police power over property devoted to public use. Public interest in a proper and suitable manner is an adequate legislative standard. Conditions on existing grants bind after due process.

### Case: *PLDT v. NTC*, G.R. No. 88404, October 18, 1990

Facts: The NTC granted a cellular newcomer provisional authority with interconnection to the public switched network, PLDT opposed as the incumbent carrier claiming vested protection of its investment and franchise area, and the regulator ordered interconnection within ninety days under the municipal telephone mandate.

Issue: The issue was whether the NTC could provisionally authorize a competing cellular operator with compelled interconnection over the incumbent objection of monopoly and taking.

Held: The Court held that the provisional grant with interconnection stood, because provisional authority is temporary, limited, non-exclusive, and revocable with the merits left for final hearing, no utility holds a constitutional right to a monopoly, and police power with R.A. No. 6849 compels interconnection for public access.

Doctrine: Provisional relief answers urgency and never decides the merits. Share sales need only Commission approval while franchise assignment needs Congress. Interconnection may be compelled and the incumbent never vetoes entry.

### Case: *Eastern Telecom v. Intl Communication*, G.R. No. 135992, July 23, 2004

Facts: An international carrier opposed a second local exchange entrant under the old service area scheme, the NTC granted the newcomer provisional authority on findings of technical and financial capability with demand growth, and the incumbent claimed exclusive territory with taking of its cross-subsidy base.

Issue: The issue was whether the NTC could grant provisional authority to a second carrier inside an area already served under a one-carrier scheme.

Held: The Court held that the grant stood, because later competition laws replaced the service area scheme with healthy competition, no authorization is ever exclusive, capability and demand findings on substantial evidence bind courts, and cross-subsidy from other services keeps less profitable areas viable.

Doctrine: A later healthy-competition law supersedes an old one-carrier rule. No prior operator owns exclusive territory. Provisional capability findings on substantial evidence are final policy judgments.

### Case: *Republic v. Republic Telephone*, G.R. No. 64888, November 28, 1996

Facts: The Bureau of Telecommunications built and operated its own telephone system overlapping an incumbent franchise area, the incumbent sued to enjoin the State entry as exclusive invasion, and the Bureau invoked directory negotiation language with free-competition policy.

Issue: The issue was whether the Government could operate its own telephone system inside an incumbent franchise area without first concluding a negotiated agreement with the prior operator.

Held: The Court held that the State entry was lawful, because the negotiation proviso was directory and not mandatory, no franchise is exclusive in character, the Government is not confined to non-commercial service, and ultra-protectionism would retard national development.

Doctrine: Directory language never conditions lawful State entry. No prior operator holds a monopoly against the Government. Free competition serves quality, technology, and service.

**Q: The LTFRB authorizes provincial bus operators to collect the P0.37 per kilometer fare plus a floating band of fifteen percent over it at their own discretion, and a later increase compounds on the collected figure. Commuters challenge the band as surrender of rate power. Is the band valid.**

A: No, because the band lets each operator fix its own effective fare and amounts to an undue delegation of the delegated rate power.
The law delegates rate fixing to the regulator under the fair and reasonable standard, the operator may propose new rates but never makes them effective without prior approval on evidence, and the regulator cannot decide in advance that unknown future rates are just.
The ruling in Pangasinan Transportation v. PSC (G.R. No. 47065, 1940) roots rate control in police power that the agency must actually exercise instead of surrendering to carriers.
The band therefore falls as illegal and invalid while the base authorized fare survives until lawfully changed.

**Q: The regulator approves a provisional rate increase without hearing on urgent public need, publishes notice to affected parties, and opens the ratification hearing on the fifteenth day. The utility collects the provisional rate meanwhile. Was the procedure lawful.**

A: Yes, because provisional rates are temporary and adjustable and the law expressly allows approval without necessity of any hearing with a hearing within fifteen days after.
The grant must publish notice to the affected territory, must convene the hearing to ratify, change, modify, or alter the provisional figure on public interest, and must fold the provisional collections into the definitive rate after final hearing.
The ruling in PLDT v. NTC (G.R. No. 88404, 1990) treats provisional relief as limited, non-exclusive, and revocable with the merits reserved for the main case.
The collections therefore stand pending the hearing outcome with refund or adjustment to follow the final rate.

**Q: An electric utility books its income tax payments as operating expenses, asks a return on that item, and defends the entry as a cost of doing business. Consumers oppose the pass-through. Must the regulator allow the item.**

A: No, because income tax never forms part of operating expenses and its burden stays with the taxpayer alone.
Operating expenses must be reasonable, recurring, and attributable to production of the service so the utility recoups prudent costs and earns a return above them, while income tax is an excise on the privilege of earning that benefits no customer and yields no service revenue.
The return cases from Republic v. Meralco (G.R. Nos. 141314 and 141369, 2002) fix the fair-return-on-property standard with expenses limited to service-related prudent costs.
The regulator therefore disallows the item and sets the rate without shifting the tax to consumers.

## 3. Transfer of Properties and Shares; Unlawful Acts; Administrative Fines; Rule-Making and Quasi-Judicial Functions

> "To sell, alienate, mortgage, encumber or lease its property, franchises, certificates, privileges, or rights, or any part thereof, or merge or consolidate its property, franchise, privileges or rights, or any part thereof, with those of any other public service. The approval therein required shall be given, after notice to the public and after hearing the persons interested at a public hearing, if it be shown that there are just and reasonable grounds for making the mortgage or encumbrance, for liabilities of more than one year maturity, or the sale, alienation, lease, merger, or consolidation to be approved, and that the same are not detrimental to the public interest, and in case of sale, the date on which the same is to be consummated shall be fixed in the order of approval: Provided, however, That nothing herein contained shall be construed to prevent the transaction from being negotiated or completed before its approval or to prevent the sale, alienation, or lease by any public service of any of its property in the ordinary course of its business: Provided, finally, That the need for approval of the sale, alienation, mortgage, encumbrance or lease will be required only for properties of the public service that are used and useful in the delivery of the required public service."

Negotiation before approval is allowed while consummation without approval is void. A share sale shifts investors and needs agency leave while a franchise assignment shifts the grant and needs congressional leave. An administrative fine protects service through the agency while a criminal sentence punishes guilt through the courts.

### Case: *Raymundo v. Luneta Motor*, G.R. Nos. 39902 and 39903, November 29, 1933

Facts: Guzco Transit defaulted on truck notes secured by chattel mortgage, the creditor attached and garnished its three bus-line certificates through the Commission secretary and bought two of them at auction, and nine days after attachment the debtor privately sold all three certificates with some trucks to Raymundo.

Issue: The issue was whether certificates of public convenience are property liable to execution and which transfer prevails between the earlier auction sale and the later private sale.

Held: The Court held that the certificates are property liable to execution, because the holder may sell voluntarily with approval so the law allows involuntary taking by legal process, and the earlier attachment and auction prevailed over the later private sale made under levy.

Doctrine: Certificates carry material value and pass the beneficial-interest test for attachment. No statute exempts them from execution. The Commission may approve transfer to the execution creditor. A private sale after levy never defeats the execution sale.

### Case: *RCPI v. NTC*, G.R. No. L-68729, May 29, 1987

Facts: A telegraph franchise holder operated with certificates from the regulator, challenged NTC authority over its services and new authorizations to competitors in the same field, and claimed its legislative franchise already authorized operation with exclusivity against entrants.

Issue: The issue was whether a legislative franchise exempts the holder from NTC certificate jurisdiction and blocks authority granted to another operator for the same area.

Held: The Court held that the franchise never exempts the holder, because the franchise and the certificate are distinct, every grant is non-exclusive in character, and the regulator keeps jurisdiction to authorize capable newcomers where public interest so requires.

Doctrine: A franchise answers the sovereign grant while a certificate answers regulatory permission. No franchise blocks a newcomer certificate. The regulator reaches every operator within its field.

### Case: *Phil Rabbit v. Gabatin*, G.R. No. L-24472, July 31, 1968

Facts: A bus applicant sought new certificates over incumbent opposition on a route with unserved passengers, the Commission found need with applicant capability on the evidence, and the incumbent invoked the prior operator rule with attacks on the findings.

Issue: The issue was whether the Commission could grant new certificates over prior-operator opposition and whether its findings of need and capability on the evidence would stand.

Held: The Court held that the grant stood, because public interest defeats the prior operator rule, findings on substantial evidence bind the Court, and credibility and preponderance belong to the Commission and not to de novo review.

Doctrine: The prior operator rule yields to proven public need. Substantial-evidence findings are final. Courts never substitute discretion on facts. Abuse of power is the only key to reversal.

**Q: A bus company sells forty-five percent of its subscribed capital to a single buyer in two connected deeds without Commission approval and books the transfer. The buyer demands recognition as stockholder. Must the company register the buyer.**

A: No, because every transfer vesting more than forty per centum of subscribed capital in one transferee without prior approval is void and unregistrable.
The law counts connected previous sales together so structuring in tranches never evades the ceiling, and the corporation may not book what the statute voids.
The ruling in PLDT v. NTC (G.R. No. 88404, 1990) applies the Sec. 20(h) approval rule to share transfers with only the agency empowered to allow them.
The buyer therefore holds nothing registrable and the company faces sanction if it keeps the entry.

**Q: A utility sells ten percent of its stock to an alien and Filipino holdings fall to fifty-five percent of capital. The parties call the sale private and internal. What follows for the sale and the certificate.**

A: The sale is void and of no effect and independently supplies cause to cancel the certificate.
The law voids every alien transfer that in itself or with connected previous sales drops Philippine citizens below sixty per centum, and the same transfer expressly warrants cancellation of the authority to operate.
The constitutional sixty percent Filipino ownership rule in Art. XII Sec. 11 anchors the statutory floor construed in Gamboa v. Teves (G.R. No. 176579, 2011) with full beneficial ownership.
The Commission therefore strikes the transfer from the books and opens cancellation while the parties renegotiate a compliant Filipino-majority structure.

**Q: The NTC fines a broadcast station P100,000 per day for operating after its provisional authority lapsed, without any prior notice or hearing, and the station claims the fine needs a criminal case in court. Is the fine valid.**

A: The fine as imposed is void, though the NTC holds the fining power and no criminal case is needed.
The law empowers the successor agency to fine for violation of certificate terms after due notice and hearing within the P5,000 to P2,000,000 daily range, and such proceedings are administrative and regulatory rather than penal with prescription under Act No. 3326 instead of court rules.
Temporary permits never bar fines for post-expiry operation, yet notice and hearing remain indispensable to the quasi-judicial act.
The NTC therefore re-issues the fine only after proper notice and hearing, with refund, suspension until payment, and court enforcement available as cumulative remedies.

## Exam Traps

1. A fine punishes the breach while revocation ends the privilege, and the two never exclude each other.
2. Contumacious breach draws cancellation while a single unconnected misuse never cancels the innocent certificate.
3. Temporary stoppage from causes beyond control never cancels, since certificates carry heavy invested capital.
4. A hearing defect is cured by full litigation on reconsideration, so later process heals earlier want of notice.
5. The NTC fines, regulates, and issues certificates, yet only Congress repeals a legislative franchise.
6. Cancellation of a broadcast license is a death sentence on speech, so strict scrutiny with the least restrictive means governs.
7. Police power fixes rates while courts judge only reasonableness, so the forum follows the question asked.
8. A fare band at operator discretion is an undue subdelegation, and compounding on collected figures doubles the defect.
9. Deregulated sectors stay deregulated, so Sec. 16(c) never revives rate control over shipping, telecom, oil, power, or BOT grants.
10. Private-business profits join the computation while income tax never does, since only service costs earn a return.
11. Provisional rates answer urgency without hearing while final rates answer merit after hearing within fifteen days.
12. Overcollection draws refund even where the rate was once approved but offends higher law.
13. Negotiation before approval is allowed while consummation without approval is void, and ordinary-course sales need no leave.
14. Only used-and-useful property needs transfer approval, so the test is service use rather than ownership alone.
15. Connected sales count together toward the forty percent ceiling, and structuring in tranches never evades it.
16. An alien drop below sixty percent is void and independently cancels the certificate.
17. Share sales need agency approval while franchise assignment needs congressional sanction, since the corporation owns the grant.
18. Certificates are privileges against the State yet property against creditors, so revocation and execution coexist.
19. Sec. 17 investigates without hearing while Sec. 21 fines only after hearing, so the power invoked decides the process.
20. One agency may investigate, prosecute, adjudicate, and make rules together without violating due process.
21. Primary jurisdiction defers to competence while franchise scope and fraud stay with ordinary courts.
22. Substantial-evidence findings bind the Court, and only shown abuse of power opens reversal.
