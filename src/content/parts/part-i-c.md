## 1. Loss

> Unless otherwise provided by the policy, an insurer is liable for a loss of which a peril insured against was the proximate cause, although a peril not contemplated by the contract may have been a remote cause of the loss; but he is not liable for a loss of which the peril insured against was only a remote cause.

> — Sec. 86, Insurance Code (RA 10607)

| Element/Requisite | Content |
|---|---|
| Proximate cause pays | Insured peril as efficient, predominant cause: liability even if an uncontemplated peril was a remote cause |
| Remote insured peril does not pay | Insured peril merely remote while another peril proximately caused the loss: no liability unless policy provides otherwise |
| Rescue extension | Loss in rescuing the thing from an insured peril, or exposure to an uninsured peril in the course of rescue depriving possession, is covered (Sec. 87) |
| Excepted peril, but-for test | Loss that would not have occurred but for a specially excepted peril is excepted, though the immediate cause was not excepted (Sec. 88) |
| Willful act bars | Loss caused by willful act or connivance of the insured: no liability (Sec. 89, first clause) |
| Negligence never bars | Negligence of insured, agents, or others does not exonerate (Sec. 89, second clause) |

Distinction table: causation direction.

| Question | Sec. 86 pays | Sec. 88 excludes |
|---|---|---|
| Who invokes | Insured proving covered loss | Insurer proving the exception |
| Test | Was the insured peril the proximate cause | Would the loss not have occurred but for the excepted peril |
| Remote peril effect | Uncontemplated remote peril is ignored | Non-excepted immediate cause is ignored |

Distinction table: loss classes in marine cases.

| Class | Trigger | Consequence |
|---|---|---|
| Actual total loss | Destruction, sinking/break-up, or damage rendering the thing valueless for the owner's purpose (Pan Malayan: germinated seed-rice; Phil Manufacturing: salve-and-repair cost above value) | Full insured value without notice of abandonment (Sec. 135, per digests) |
| Constructive total loss | Abandonment of the thing or a separately valued portion where more than three-fourths in value is lost or value reduced by more than three-fourths (Sec. 139, per digest) | Recovery on abandonment; fails where the contract is indivisible (Oriental Assurance) |
| Partial loss | Anything short of total | Indemnity for the part lost only |

### Case: *Pan Malayan Insurance Corp. v. CA (FAO)*, G.R. No. 95070, Sept 5, 1991

Facts: FAO shipped 34,122 bags of IR-36 seed-rice on a barge that sank. Survey showed 23,510 bags wetted 10 to 15 percent and 983 bags shortlanded or missing, with further wetted bags found on sorting. The insurer denied the claim on partial-damage grounds.

Issue: Whether there was a total loss of the shipment.

Held: There was actual total loss. Contact with water activated germination and rendered the seeds valueless for FAO's planting purpose. The sinking also deprived FAO of possession at destination.

Doctrine: Complete physical destruction is not essential. Damage that renders the thing valueless for the owner's purpose is actual total loss. The full insured value is then due without notice of abandonment.

> Exam trap: Do not argue abandonment validity where actual total loss is found; the right to the whole insurance is absolute and abandonment drops out.

### Case: *Oriental Assurance Corp. v. CA (Panama Sawmill)*, G.R. No. 94052, Aug 9, 1991

Facts: Apitong logs totalling 1,208 pieces (2,000 cubic meters) were insured for P1,000,000 against total loss only and loaded on two barges. Rough seas caused the loss of 497 pieces carried on one barge. The lower courts treated each barge separately and found constructive total loss.

Issue: Whether the per-barge loss sustains recovery under a total-loss-only policy.

Held: No. One premium for one undivided shipment with no separate valuation or separate insurance means one indivisible contract. For that reason 497 of 1,208 pieces (41.45 percent) is neither actual nor constructive total loss.

Doctrine: Indivisibility follows intention, premium unity, and absence of separate valuation. The Sec. 139 three-fourths test therefore runs on the whole shipment.

> Exam trap: Two barges do not make two contracts; check separate valuation and separate premium before dividing the cargo.

### Case: *Philippine Manufacturing Co. v. Union Insurance Society of Canton*, G.R. No. L-16473, Nov 22, 1921

Facts: The lighter Philmaco sank in Manila Bay in a typhoon during the policy term. Salve and repair cost substantially equaled original cost and policy value.

Issue: Whether there was absolute (actual) total loss though the hull was raised and rebuilt.

Held: There was actual total loss. Lying at the bay bottom, the lighter was of no value to the owner. Repair cost above value makes repair practically impossible.

Doctrine: Valuelessness to the owner under existing conditions, not disintegration of parts, is the test. Foreign marine law that is unpleaded and unproved is not applied.

**2017 Bar Q: Absolute Timber Co. (ATC) has been engaged in the logging business in Isabela. To secure one of its shipments of logs to be transported by Andok Shipping Co., ATC purchased a marine policy with an "all risks" provision. Because of a strong typhoon then hitting Northern Luzon, the vessel sank and the shipment of logs was totally lost. ATC filed its claim, but the insurer denied the claim on several grounds, namely: (1) the vessel had not been seaworthy; (2) the vessel's crew had lacked sufficient training; (3) the improper loading of the logs on only one side of the vessel had led to the tilting of the ship to that side during the stormy voyage; and (4) the extremely bad weather had been a fortuitous event. ATC now seeks your legal advice to know if its claim was sustainable. What is your advice. Explain your answer. (3 percent)**

A: The claim is sustainable. An all-risks marine policy covers all fortuitous losses proximately caused by perils of the sea unless expressly excepted, and the typhoon sinking is the proximate cause of total loss.
Sec. 86 holds the insurer liable where the insured peril was the proximate cause though an uncontemplated peril was remote. Sec. 89 provides that negligence of the insured or its agents does not exonerate. Under Pan Malayan a sunken cargo valueless to the owner is actual total loss payable in full.
Defenses 1 to 3 sound in unseaworthiness, manning, and loading negligence, which Sec. 89 keeps inside coverage. Defense 4 concedes that the insured peril itself caused the sinking. No excepted peril was invoked under the Sec. 88 but-for test.
Denial is improper. The insurer is liable for the total loss.

Complements table for §1.

| Item | Content |
|---|---|
| Measure on actual total loss | Full insured or insurable value without notice of abandonment |
| Constructive-loss math | More than three-fourths in value lost or value reduced (Sec. 139, per digest) |
| Fraudulent overclaim | Padded claim voids the policy; insurer proves fraud after insured's prima facie case (United Merchants, cross-listed §4) |

## 2. Notice of Loss

> In case of loss upon an insurance against fire, an insurer is exonerated, if written notice thereof be not given to him by an insured, or some person entitled to the benefit of the insurance, without unnecessary delay. For other non-life insurance, the Commissioner may specify the period for the submission of the notice of loss.

> — Sec. 90, Insurance Code (RA 10607)

| Element/Requisite | Content |
|---|---|
| Fire: written notice, no unnecessary delay | Given by the insured or any person entitled to the benefit; non-compliance exonerates the fire insurer |
| Other non-life: Commissioner-set period | The 90-day-style deadline is regulatory, not in the section; check the Commissioner's issuance |
| Proof is best-evidence only | Preliminary proof needs the best evidence in the insured's power at the time, not court-grade proof (Sec. 91) |
| Remediable defects waived | Defects the insured could remedy are waived unless the insurer specifies them without unnecessary delay as grounds of objection (Sec. 92) |
| Delay waived two ways | Insurer-caused delay, or failure to object promptly and specifically on delay ground, waives delay (Sec. 93) |
| Third-party certificates | Reasonable diligence to procure suffices; on refusal without just grounds of disbelief, furnish reasonable evidence of that (Sec. 94) |

Distinction table: notice vs proof.

| Point | Notice (Sec. 90) | Proof of loss (Secs. 91, 94) |
|---|---|---|
| Function | Alerts the insurer that loss happened | Documents the loss for adjustment |
| Standard | Written, without unnecessary delay (fire) | Best evidence in hand; diligence for third-party papers |
| Insurer silence effect | Delay objection waived if not prompt and specific (Sec. 93) | Defect objection waived if not specified without delay (Sec. 92) |

Case card: none assigned. Do not cite an unassigned case for notice doctrine.

Historic Bar question: gap. The 2016-2025 file carries no verbatim question on notice or proof of loss. The section is tested through the Code rules above.

**Q: A fire policy requires written notice within 15 days. The insured notifies on day 40 without justification, then cures a defective proof after the insurer specifies the defect late. Run Secs. 90, 92, and 93 and conclude which omission exonerates the insurer and which omission it waived.**

A: The insurer is exonerated for the late fire notice under Sec. 90, because forty days without justification exceeds without unnecessary delay, unless the delay was insurer-caused or went unobjected under one of the two Sec. 93 waivers. The proof defect specified late is waived under Sec. 92, since a defect the insurer failed to specify without delay cannot later defeat the claim. The notice delay therefore bars recovery unless waived, while the late-specified defects do not bar it.

Complements table for §2.

| Item | Content |
|---|---|
| Who may give notice | Insured or any person entitled to the benefit of the insurance |
| Form for fire | Written |
| Proof measure | Best evidence in power at the time, not trial-grade proof |

## 3. Collateral Source Rule

> Authority note: the syllabus assigns no Code section letter to this item and no Batch-1 case; the rule below is the chapter rule (p. 219), stated without an invented holding.

| Element/Requisite | Content |
|---|---|
| The rule | Benefits received from a source independent of the insurer and the wrongdoer do not reduce the insurer's liability on the policy |
| Why it holds | Insurance pays on its own contract promise; the collateral benefit is not policy proceeds and not the wrongdoer's payment |
| Indemnity limit preserved | No double recovery results because total recovery is still capped at the loss; the paying insurer takes its recourse through subrogation, not deduction |
| Mirror in double insurance | Secs. 95-96 deduction and ratable contribution apply only between co-insurers of the same person, subject, and interest, never against an independent collateral source |

Distinction table: collateral source vs double insurance vs subrogation.

| Mechanism | Reduces insurer payment | Why |
|---|---|---|
| Collateral source | No | Independent promisor, separate contract |
| Double insurance (Secs. 95-96) | Yes, by deduction and contribution | Same person, same subject and interest |
| Subrogation (Art. 2207) | Reimburses after payment | Shifts the paid loss to the wrongdoer |

Historic Bar question: gap — the 2016-2025 file carries no verbatim collateral-source question.

**Q: The insured's employer pays sick-leave benefits for the same confinement the health insurer covers. The insurer deducts the sick-leave amount. Decide, then explain where the insurer's real recourse lies.**

A: The deduction is improper because the sick-leave pay comes from a source independent of the insurer, so the collateral source rule requires the health policy to pay in full with total recovery capped at the loss. Employer benefits and policy proceeds answer different promises, and Secs. 95-96 deduction and contribution apply only to same-person same-interest co-insurance. The insurer must therefore pay the policy in full, and any recourse runs through subrogation against the party liable for the loss, never by deduction from the insured.

Complements table for §3.

| Item | Content |
|---|---|
| Test of independence | Different promisor, different contract or status, not the insurer or the wrongdoer |
| Cap | Aggregate recovery never exceeds the loss |
| Remedy direction | Insurer pays, then is subrogated; it does not deduct |

## 4. Claims Settlement

> No insurance company doing business in the Philippines shall refuse, without just cause, to pay or settle claims arising under coverages provided by its policies, nor shall any such company engage in unfair claim settlement practices. Any of the following acts by an insurance company, if committed without just cause and performed with such frequency as to indicate a general business practice, shall constitute unfair claim settlement practices: (1) Knowingly misrepresenting to claimants pertinent facts or policy provisions relating to coverage at issue; (2) Failing to acknowledge with reasonable promptness pertinent communications with respect to claims arising under its policies; (3) Failing to adopt and implement reasonable standards for the prompt investigation of claims arising under its policies; (4) Not attempting in good faith to effectuate prompt, fair and equitable settlement of claims submitted in which liability has become reasonably clear; or (5) Compelling policyholders to institute suits to recover amounts due under its policies by offering without justifiable reason substantially less than the amounts ultimately recovered in suits brought by them.

> — Sec. 247(a), Insurance Code (RA 10607)

| Element/Requisite | Content |
|---|---|
| Two qualifiers for every act | Without just cause plus frequency indicating general business practice; an isolated lapse is not the offense |
| Life clock (Sec. 248) | Proceeds immediately on maturity; death proceeds within 60 days after claim presentation and proof of death; delay interest at twice the Monetary Board ceiling unless the claim is fraudulent |
| Non-life clock (Sec. 249) | Pay within 30 days after receipt of proof of loss plus ascertainment by agreement or arbitration; if no ascertainment within 60 days of receipt, pay within 90 days of receipt; same double-ceiling interest unless fraudulent |
| Unreasonable-denial finding (Sec. 250) | Commissioner or court must make the finding in any enforcement litigation; damages are attorney's fees and expenses plus double-ceiling interest from the day after the Sec. 248/249 deadline until full satisfaction; missing the statutory deadline is prima facie unreasonable delay |
| Fraudulent claim (Sec. 251) | Presenting or supporting a fraudulent claim is unlawful: fine up to twice the amount claimed or two years imprisonment or both |

Distinction table: life vs non-life payment.

| Point | Life (Sec. 248) | Non-life (Sec. 249) |
|---|---|---|
| Trigger | Maturity; for death, presentation of claim plus proof of death | Receipt of proof of loss |
| Deadline | Immediately; 60 days for death proceeds | 30 days after receipt plus ascertainment; 90 days if no ascertainment within 60 |
| Common penalty | Double-ceiling interest for delay, excused only by fraudulent claim | Same |

Distinction table: unfair practice vs unreasonable denial vs fraudulent claim.

| Concept | Actor | Consequence |
|---|---|---|
| Unfair claim settlement practice (Sec. 247) | Insurer, general business practice | Each instance a separate violation; suspension or revocation of certificate of authority |
| Unreasonable denial or withholding (Sec. 250) | Insurer in litigated enforcement | Attorney's fees, expenses, double-ceiling interest |
| Fraudulent claim (Sec. 251) | Claimant or supporter | Fine, imprisonment, or both; claim fails |

### Case: *United Merchants Corp. v. Country Bankers Insurance Corp.*, G.R. No. 198588, July 11, 2012 (cross-listed from Part I-B)

Facts: The insured filed a fire claim that the insurer found to be padded. The insurer denied payment and invoked fraud as a defense to coverage.

Issue: Whether a padded claim voids the policy and who carries the burden of proving the fraud defense.

Held: A padded claim voids the policy for fraud. After the insured presents a prima facie case, the insurer carries the burden of proving the exception. Fraud is both a defense to payment and, when presented as a claim, the Sec. 251 offense. The interest penalties in Secs. 248 to 250 never run where refusal rests on a fraudulent claim.

Doctrine: Fraud must be proved and is never presumed. A proved padded-claim finding voids coverage and excuses the payment clocks.

> Exam trap: Fraud excuses the Secs. 248-250 interest, but the insurer must prove the fraud; a bare allegation neither stops the clock nor voids the policy.

Historic Bar question: gap — the 2016-2025 file carries no verbatim unfair-practices or payment-clock question.

**Q: A non-life insurer receives complete proof of loss on March 1, neither agrees on the amount nor arbitrates within 60 days, and pays on day 120, blaming heavy volume. The insured sues for the proceeds plus damages. Decide interest and damages.**

A: The insurer owes double-ceiling interest from May 31, the day after the 90-day Sec. 249 deadline fixed at May 30 where ascertainment defaulted, until full satisfaction, because heavy volume is not just cause and missing the deadline is prima facie unreasonable delay under Sec. 250. Payment on day 120 is therefore 30 days of delay with no fraud defense pleaded. The insured recovers the proceeds plus double-ceiling interest, attorney's fees, and proven expenses.

Complements table for §4.

| Item | Content |
|---|---|
| Complaint evidence (Sec. 247(b)) | Valid complaint numbers and types plus the Commissioner's comparative experience are admissible |
| Per-instance rule (Sec. 247(c)) | Each noncompliance is a separate violation after notice and hearing |
| Fraud cutoff | All payment clocks and interest stop where refusal rests on a fraudulent claim |

## 5. Prescriptive Period

> A condition, stipulation, or agreement in any policy of insurance, limiting the time for commencing an action thereunder to a period of less than one (1) year from the time when the cause of action accrues, is void.

> — Sec. 63, Insurance Code (RA 10607)

| Element/Requisite | Content |
|---|---|
| Floor, not ceiling | Parties may stipulate a suit-time period; only a period below one year from accrual is void |
| Lawful stipulation enforced | A one-year-or-longer stipulation governs; file within it or lose the action |
| No-stipulation rule | With no valid stipulation, the action is governed by the general law on prescription of actions |
| Accrual | The one-year minimum runs from when the cause of action accrues, not from loss alone; the chapter treats denial or ascertainment dispute as the accrual reference for enforcement suits |

Distinction table: three prescriptive situations.

| Situation | Rule | Effect |
|---|---|---|
| Stipulation below one year from accrual | Void under Sec. 63 | Clause struck; action proceeds under the lawful period |
| Stipulation of one year or more | Valid | Must sue within the stipulated period |
| No stipulation | General prescription law | Period runs from accrual under applicable law |

Historic Bar question: gap — the 2016-2025 file carries no verbatim prescriptive-period question.

**Q: A fire policy requires suit within six months of the fire. The insured sues in month eight after a denied claim. The insurer pleads the six-month clause. Decide.**

A: The six-month suit clause is void under Sec. 63 because it falls below the one-year-from-accrual floor, reckoned from accrual which here postdates the fire, so the insurer's plea fails. The suit filed in month eight is proper, and the insurer must defend on the merits.

Complements table for §5.

| Item | Content |
|---|---|
| Void line | Anything below one year from accrual |
| Valid line | One year or longer, enforced as written |
| Accrual anchor | Enforcement cause of action, not the bare date of loss |

## 6. Subrogation

> If the plaintiff's property has been insured, and he has received indemnity from the insurance company for the injury or loss arising out of the wrong or breach of contract complained of, the insurance company shall be subrogated to the rights of the insured against the wrongdoer or the person who has violated the contract. If the amount paid by the insurance company does not fully cover the injury or loss, the aggrieved party shall be entitled to recover the deficiency from the person causing the loss or injury.

> — Art. 2207, Civil Code

| Element/Requisite | Content |
|---|---|
| Payment of the claim | Subrogation accrues simply upon payment; no privity, assignment, or written transfer needed (Delsan; Cebu Shipyard) |
| Insured loss from wrong or breach | The defendant must be the wrongdoer or the contract-breaker vis-a-vis the insured (carrier, arrastre operator, tortfeasor) |
| Substitution, not duplication | Insurer steps into the insured's shoes against that defendant; insured keeps only the deficiency (Art. 2207, second sentence) |
| Amount cap | Recovery limited to the amount paid; excess over loss held and deficiency stays claimable by the aggrieved party |
| No double-insurance shortcut | Where two policies cover different persons with distinct interests, there is no double insurance and contribution (Sec. 96(e)) does not apply (Malayan v Phil First) |
| Policy presentation | Subrogation receipt suffices to prove relation and amount; policy needed only where multi-stage handling hides where damage occurred (Delsan; Asian Terminals) |

Distinction table: subrogation vs contribution vs reimbursement.

| Mechanism | Source | Direction |
|---|---|---|
| Subrogation (Art. 2207) | Payment of insured's loss | Insurer vs wrongdoer or contract-breaker, up to amount paid |
| Contribution (Sec. 96(e)) | Double insurance overpayment | Co-insurer vs co-insurer, ratably by respective liabilities |
| Deficiency recovery (Art. 2207) | Partial indemnity | Aggrieved party vs person causing loss, for the uncovered balance |

Distinction table: when the policy must be presented.

| Situation | Policy needed | Authority |
|---|---|---|

### Case: *Malayan Insurance Co. v. Philippines First Insurance and Reputable Forwarder*, G.R. No. 184300, July 11, 2012

Facts: Wyeth's infant-formula cargo was hijacked in transit. Philippines First paid Wyeth P2,133,257 under its marine policy and sued Reputable, the private carrier bound by contract to answer for all risks including theft and force majeure. Reputable impleaded Malayan, its own SR-policy insurer, which invoked the other-insurance and over-insurance clauses.

Issue: Whether there is double insurance making the other-insurance and ratable-contribution clauses applicable, and whether Reputable and Malayan are solidarily liable.

Held: There is no double insurance. The two policies covered the same goods and peril but insured two different persons with distinct interests (owner versus carrier under Sec. 15). Neither clause applies and Malayan owes its full P1,000,000 cover. There is no solidary liability. Malayan answers on the policy while Reputable answers on the carriage contract.

Doctrine: Same subject plus same peril is not double insurance without the same person and the same interest. Ambiguities construe against the insurer. Distinct obligations defeat solidarity.

> Exam trap: Full same-goods same-peril overlap still fails double insurance where insured persons and interests differ; check identity of person and interest before applying Secs. 95-96.

### Case: *Delsan Transport Lines v. CA (American Home)*, G.R. No. 127897, Nov 15, 2001

Facts: Caltex's fuel-oil cargo sank with MT Maysun. American Home paid P5,096,635.57 and sued the carrier as subrogee. The carrier pleaded seaworthiness admission, missing policy, and force majeure.

Issue: Whether payment admits seaworthiness, whether the policy must be presented, and whether the carrier is liable.

Held: Payment waives the seaworthiness warranty against the assured but subrogates against the carrier. The subrogation receipt suffices and the policy need not be presented on a single known leg. The carrier failed to rebut presumed negligence (PAGASA readings versus claimed 18 to 20-foot waves) and is liable.

Doctrine: Art. 2207 subrogation is equitable and automatic on payment. The receipt proves relation and amount. Carrier negligence is presumed from unexplained sinking.

> Exam trap: Payment cuts off the insurer vs its own assured on seaworthiness but opens the insurer vs the carrier; do not carry the waiver across to the subrogation suit.

### Case: *Cebu Shipyard and Engineering Works v. William Lines*, G.R. No. 132607, May 5, 1999

Facts: The vessel was in the repairer's custody and was lost through its negligence. The repairer invoked a liability cap and a policy exclusion.

Issue: Whether the paying insurer is subrogated to the insured's rights against the negligent repairer and whether the cap and exclusion defenses hold.

Held: The paying insurer is subrogated under Art. 2207 to the insured's rights against the negligent repairer. The exclusion defense fails against proved negligence. An unconscionable cap will not be enforced.

Doctrine: Subrogation reaches any wrongdoer, including a custodian-repairer. Contractual caps and exclusions do not survive the subrogee's proved-negligence case.

### Case: *Asian Terminals v. Malayan Insurance*, G.R. No. 171406, Apr 4, 2011

Facts: The insurer as subrogee sued the arrastre operator. The defendant demanded presentation of the policy.

Issue: Whether the subrogation receipt alone proves the claim without presentation of the policy.

Held: The subrogation receipt alone establishes the insurer-insured relation and the amount paid. The policy becomes necessary only where multi-stage handling obscures where the damage occurred.

Doctrine: Accrual on payment plus receipt sufficiency is the standing rule. The multi-stage exception is narrow.

**2025 Bar Q: X obtained an insurance policy for her Porsche Carrera GT from XYZ Insurance Company. The same car was insured by her creditor-mortgagee, Y, with ABC Insurance Company. When the car was damaged because of an accident, X filed a claim with XYZ Insurance Company. The latter denied the claim since it discovered that the car was also insured with ABC Insurance Company. According to XYZ Insurance Company, double insurance is prohibited by law. Decide with reason(s).**

A: Denial is improper. Double insurance exists only where the same person is insured by several insurers separately in respect to the same subject and interest, and X and Y are different persons with distinct interests (owner vs creditor-mortgagee).
Sec. 95 defines double insurance, and under Malayan v Phil First the same goods and the same peril for different persons with distinct insurable interests is not double insurance, so the other-insurance and contribution clauses do not apply.
X's policy answers her ownership interest while Y's answers the mortgage-credit interest. XYZ must pay X's claim and, having paid, is subrogated under Art. 2207 against the party at fault for the accident. Any over-indemnity is adjusted by Sec. 96 deduction and ratable contribution.
XYZ is liable on the policy. Double insurance is regulated and apportioned, not prohibited.

## Exam Traps

1. Proximate direction matters. An insured peril that is proximate pays. An insured peril that is merely remote does not pay. Do not invert Sec. 86.
2. Sec. 88 runs the other way. A but-for excepted peril excludes even where the immediate cause was covered. Do not merge it with Sec. 86.
3. Negligence never exonerates under Sec. 89. Only willful act or connivance exonerates. Unseaworthiness, bad manning, and bad loading sound in negligence.
4. Actual total loss needs valuelessness for purpose, not disintegration. Germinated seeds and a sunken lighter both qualify.
5. Indivisibility is tested by separate valuation, separate insurance, and separate premium, not by separate barges.
6. Fire notice must be written and without unnecessary delay. Other non-life deadlines come from the Commissioner, not the section.
7. Proof is best-evidence, never court-grade. Demanding trial proof misstates Sec. 91.
8. Defects are waived unless specified without delay. Delay is waived unless objected to promptly and specifically or caused by the insurer. Keep Secs. 92 and 93 apart.
9. A third-party certificate is diligence-based. Refusal without just disbelief grounds is cured by reasonable evidence under Sec. 94.
10. Collateral benefits do not reduce the policy. Deduction and contribution belong only to same-person same-interest double insurance.
11. All five Sec. 247(a) acts need both without just cause and general-practice frequency. One isolated lapse is not the offense.
12. Life 60 days runs from claim plus proof of death. Non-life 30 days runs from proof plus ascertainment and 90 days without it. Do not swap triggers.
13. Missing the statutory clock is prima facie unreasonable delay, not conclusive proof. The Sec. 250 finding still must be made.
14. Fraud excuses interest but must be proved. A bare allegation neither stops the clock nor voids the policy.
15. Sub-one-year suit limits are void from accrual, not from loss. Measure the floor at accrual.
16. Subrogation accrues on payment with no assignment needed. Demanding one misstates Art. 2207.
17. Payment waives seaworthiness versus the assured but preserves subrogation versus the carrier. Do not carry the waiver across suits.
18. The receipt suffices except in multi-stage handling gaps. Demanding the policy on a single known leg misstates Delsan.
19. Same goods plus same peril is not double insurance without same person and same interest. Check both identities before applying Secs. 95 to 96.
20. Insurer and wrongdoer are never solidary. Their obligations have distinct sources.
