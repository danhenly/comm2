## 1. Philippine Competition Commission

> "The State shall regulate or prohibit monopolies when the public interest so requires. No combinations in restraint of trade or unfair competition shall be allowed."

> "To implement the national competition policy and attain the objectives and purposes of this Act, an independent quasi-judicial body is hereby created, which shall be known as the Philippine Competition Commission (PCC), hereinafter referred to as the Commission, and which shall be organized within sixty (60) days after the effectivity of this Act. Upon establishment of the Commission, Executive Order No. 45 designating the Department of Justice as the Competition Authority is hereby amended. The Office for Competition (OFC) under the Office of the Secretary of Justice shall however be retained, with its powers and functions modified pursuant to Section 13 of this Chapter."

> "The Commission shall be an attached agency to the Office of the President."

> "The Commission shall have original and primary jurisdiction over the enforcement and implementation of the provisions of this Act, and its implementing rules and regulations. The Commission shall exercise the following powers and functions: (a) Conduct inquiry, investigate, and hear and decide on cases involving any violation of this Act and other existing competition laws motu proprio or upon receipt of a verified complaint from an interested party or upon referral by the concerned regulatory agency, and institute the appropriate civil or criminal proceedings; (b) Review proposed mergers and acquisitions, determine thresholds for notification, determine the requirements and procedures for notification, and upon exercise of its powers to review, prohibit mergers and acquisitions that will substantially prevent, restrict, or lessen competition in the relevant market;"

> "(d) Upon finding, based on substantial evidence, that an entity has entered into an anti-competitive agreement or has abused its dominant position after due notice and hearing, stop or redress the same, by applying remedies, such as, but not limited to, issuance of injunctions, requirement of divestment, and disgorgement of excess profits under such reasonable parameters that shall be prescribed by the rules and regulations implementing this Act; (e) Conduct administrative proceedings, impose sanctions, fines or penalties for any noncompliance with or breach of this Act and its implementing rules and regulations (IRR) and punish for contempt; (f) Issue subpoena duces tecum and subpoena ad testificandum to require the production of books, records, or other documents or data which relate to any matter relevant to the investigation and personal appearance before the Commission, summon witnesses, administer oaths, and issue interim orders such as show cause orders and cease and desist orders after due notice and hearing in accordance with the rules and regulations implementing this Act;"

> "(h) Issue adjustment or divestiture orders including orders for corporate reorganization or divestment in the manner and under such terms and conditions as may be prescribed in the rules and regulations implementing this Act. Adjustment or divestiture orders, which are structural remedies, should only be imposed: (1) Where there is no equally effective behavioral remedy; or (2) Where any equally effective behavioral remedy would be more burdensome for the enterprise concerned than the structural remedy. Changes to the structure of an enterprise as it existed before the infringement was committed would only be proportionate to the substantial risk of a lasting or repeated infringement that derives from the very structure of the enterprise;"

> "(a) Administrative Fines. – In any investigation under Chapter III, Sections 14 and 15, and Chapter IV, Sections 17 and 20 of this Act, after due notice and hearing, the Commission may impose the following schedule of administrative fines on any entity found to have violated the said sections: First offense: Fine of up to one hundred million pesos (P100,000,000.00); Second offense: Fine of not less than one hundred million pesos (P100,000,000.00) but not more than two hundred fifty million pesos (P250,000,000.00)."

> "In fixing the amount of the fine, the Commission shall have regard to both the gravity and the duration of the violation."

> "(b) Failure to Comply With an Order of the Commission. – An entity which fails or refuses to comply with a ruling, order or decision issued by the Commission shall pay a penalty of not less than fifty thousand pesos (P50,000.00) up to two million pesos (P2,000,000.00) for each violation and a similar amount of penalty for each day thereafter until the said entity fully complies. Provided that these fines shall only accrue daily beginning forty-five (45) days from the time that the said decision, order or ruling was received. (c) Supply of Incorrect or Misleading Information. – The Commission may likewise impose upon any entity fines of up to one million pesos (P1,000,000.00) where, intentionally or negligently, they supply incorrect or misleading information in any document, application or other paper filed with or submitted to the Commission or supply incorrect or misleading information in an application for a binding ruling, a proposal for a consent judgment, proceedings relating to a show cause order, or application for modification of the Commission's ruling, order or approval, as the case may be. (d) Any other violations not specifically penalized under the relevant provisions of this Act shall be penalized by a fine of not less than fifty thousand pesos (P50,000.00) up to two million pesos (P2,000,000.00)."

> "Provided that the schedule of fines indicated in this section shall be increased by the Commission every five (5) years to maintain their real value from the time it was set."

| Element or Requisite | Content |
|---|---|
| Independence | The Commission is an independent quasi-judicial body, so attachment to the Office of the President never subjects its adjudication to executive direction and its members enjoy security of tenure removable only for just cause |
| Original and primary jurisdiction | The Commission takes every competition issue first, and where competition and noncompetition issues mix it still decides after consulting the sector regulator and receiving its opinion |
| Own-motion and complaint inquiry | The Commission acts on its own motion, on a verified complaint from an interested party, or on referral from a regulator, and no other law enforcement agency may run a parallel competition inquiry |
| Preliminary inquiry clock | Every preliminary inquiry ends within ninety days from the verified complaint, the referral, or the own-motion opening, either by closure for no violation or by resolution to open full investigation |
| Interim protection | After due notice and hearing the Commission may order temporary cessation of acts that would materially harm consumers or competition while the case runs |
| Criminal referral | Where evidence warrants, the Commission files the criminal complaint with the Department of Justice, which prosecutes before the proper court under the Revised Rules of Criminal Procedure |
| Merger review | The Commission sets thresholds and procedures, reviews notified deals, and prohibits or conditions deals that substantially lessen competition |
| Redress after hearing | On substantial evidence after due notice and hearing the Commission stops or redresses the violation with injunctions, divestment, and disgorgement of excess profits under the implementing rules |
| Structural remedy limit | Divestiture and reorganization issue only where no equally effective behavioral remedy exists or where the behavioral option burdens the enterprise more, and only in proportion to a lasting or repeated infringement risk rooted in the structure itself |
| Contempt | Misconduct that seriously interrupts proceedings, including willful defiance of a lawful summons or refusal to testify, draws up to thirty days imprisonment or up to P100,000.00 fine or both |
| Tripled fines | Violations touching basic necessities and prime commodities under R.A. No. 7581 as amended draw triple the fine from the Commission or the courts |
| Five-year escalation | The Commission raises the whole fine schedule every five years to preserve real value |

Independence shields adjudication while attachment serves administration, so the President never dictates outcomes. Redress cures the market while fines punish the violator, so injunctions with disgorgement may run alongside monetary penalties. Behavioral fixes come first while structural surgery waits, so divestiture needs a finding that gentler orders cannot work.

### Case: *Tatad v. Secretary of DOE*, G.R. No. 124360, November 5, 1997

Facts: Congress deregulated the downstream oil industry through R.A. No. 8180 while keeping a tariff differential, an inventory requirement, and a predatory pricing ban that favored the three incumbent refiners. Taxpayers assailed the statute as an unconstitutional barrier to new entrants.

Issue: The issue was whether a deregulation law that entrenches an oligopoly violates the constitutional command against monopolies and combinations in restraint of trade.

Held: The Court struck down the whole statute, because the tariff gap with the inventory burden with the toothless pricing ban walled out newcomers while freeing the three majors from price control, and the offending provisions so permeated the law that no part could survive.

Doctrine: Section 19 of Article XII is anti-trust in history and in spirit and makes competition its underlying principle. A statute that barricades entry into an oligopolized market must fall in its entirety where Congress could not have passed it without the offending props. An independent competition enforcer exists so that capture of this kind meets a forum bound to evidence and not to politics. (Full digest in Batch 3.)

**Q: The Secretary of Energy writes the Commission directing it to clear a refinery merger without review because the project is a presidential priority. The merging refiners invoke the letter and demand immediate clearance. Must the Commission obey the directive.**

A: No. The Commission must disregard the directive and must independently review the refinery merger on the merits, and the merging refiners cannot compel immediate clearance by invoking the letter.
Under R.A. No. 10667 Sec. 5 creating the Commission as an independent quasi-judicial body attached only for administration to the Office of the President, Sec. 12 granting it original and primary jurisdiction over merger review, and Sec. 31 granting sole and exclusive authority to open and resolve inquiries, applied in Tatad v. Secretary of DOE (G.R. No. 124360, November 5, 1997) which struck down executive measures entrenching an oligopoly in violation of 1987 Constitution Art. XII Sec. 19, independence means adjudication turns only on evidence after due notice and hearing and never on political direction.
Here, the Secretary of Energy ordered clearance without review because the project is a presidential priority, and the merging refiners demand immediate clearance on that basis, but attachment to the Office of the President does not authorize direction of outcomes, and a cabinet letter invoking presidential priority is neither substantial evidence nor law that can displace notification and review, so the claim that administrative attachment equals obedience fails.
The Commission therefore disregards the letter, retains jurisdiction, conducts inquiry and merger review to prohibition or conditional clearance on the merits, and any clearance issued without that review would be void for lack of jurisdiction and due process.

**Q: After due hearing the Commission finds a cement cartel and orders the members to sell two plants although a supervised price and supply commitment would restore rivalry. The cartel answers that divestiture is a listed remedy so the Commission may choose it freely. Is the divestiture order valid.**

A: No. The divestiture order is invalid and must be set aside, and the Commission must instead impose the equally effective supervised price and supply commitment that restores rivalry with less intrusion.
Under R.A. No. 10667 Sec. 12(d) allowing stoppage or redress only upon substantial evidence after due notice and hearing through injunctions, divestment and disgorgement, and Sec. 12(h) permitting adjustment or divestiture orders only where no equally effective behavioral remedy exists or where any equally effective behavioral remedy would be more burdensome, proportionate only to lasting or repeated infringement risk rooted in structure, as enforced by an independent enforcer under Tatad v. Secretary of DOE (G.R. No. 124360, November 5, 1997) pursuant to 1987 Constitution Art. XII Sec. 19, mere listing of divestiture among remedies never makes it freely electable.
Here, the Commission found a cement cartel after due hearing but ordered members to sell two plants although it conceded a supervised price and supply commitment would restore rivalry, and the cartel wrongly reads mere listing of divestiture as free choice, but the admitted effectiveness of the behavioral commitment defeats the first statutory condition and shows the order punishes structure rather than curing conduct proportionately.
The Commission therefore vacates divestiture, substitutes the effective behavioral order with monitoring and retains disgorgement or fines if warranted, reserving divestiture for proof that conduct relief cannot work or burdens more than reorganization.

## 2. Composition, Term, Qualification; Confidentiality; Remedies of the Entity

> "The Commission shall be composed of a Chairperson and four (4) Commissioners. The Chairperson and the Commissioners shall be citizens and residents of the Philippines, of good moral character, of recognized probity and independence and must have distinguished themselves professionally in public, civic or academic service in any of the following fields: economics, law, finance, commerce or engineering. They must have been in the active practice of their professions for at least ten (10) years, and must not have been candidates for any elective national or local office in the immediately preceding elections, whether regular or special: Provided, That at least one (1) shall be a member of the Philippine Bar with at least ten (10) years of experience in the active practice of law, and at least one (1) shall be an economist. The Chairperson and the Commissioners who shall have the rank equivalent of cabinet secretary and undersecretary, respectively, shall be appointed by the President."

> "The term of office of the Chairperson and the Commissioners shall be seven (7) years without reappointment. Of the first set of appointees, the Chairperson shall hold office for seven (7) years and of the first four (4) Commissioners, two (2) shall hold office for a term of seven (7) years and two (2) for a term of five (5) years. In case a vacancy occurs before the expiration of the term of office, the appointment to such vacancy shall only be for the unexpired term of the predecessor."

> "The Chairperson and the Commissioners shall enjoy security of tenure and shall not be suspended or removed from office except for just cause as provided by law."

> "The Commissioners shall not, during their tenure, hold any other office or employment. They shall not, during their tenure, directly or indirectly practice any profession, except in a teaching capacity, participate in any business, or be financially interested in any contract with, or any franchise, or special privileges granted by the government or any subdivision, agency, or instrumentality thereof, including government-owned and -controlled corporations or their subsidiaries. They shall strictly avoid conflict of interest in the conduct of their office. They shall not be qualified to run for any office in the election immediately succeeding their cessation from office: Provided, That the election mentioned hereof is not a Barangay election or a Sangguniang Kabataan election. Provided, they shall not be allowed to personally appear or practice as counsel or agent on any matter pending before the Commission for two (2) years following their cessation from office."

> "Three (3) members of the Commission shall constitute a quorum and the affirmative vote of three (3) members shall be necessary for the adoption of any rule, ruling, order, resolution, decision or other acts of the Commission."

> "Confidential business information submitted by entities, relevant to any inquiry or investigation being conducted pursuant to this Act as well as any deliberation in relation thereto, shall not, in any manner, be directly or indirectly disclosed, published, transferred, copied, or disseminated. Likewise, the Commission shall, to the extent possible, subject such information to the confidentiality rule provided under this section when it issues notices, bulletins, rulings and other documents: Provided, That the confidentiality rule shall not apply if the notifying entity consents to the disclosure, or the document or information is mandatorily required to be disclosed by law or by a valid order of a court of competent jurisdiction or of a government or regulatory agency, including an exchange. The identity of the persons who provide information to the Commission under condition of anonymity, shall remain confidential, unless such confidentiality is expressly waived by these persons."

> "Any violation of this provision shall be imposed a fine of not less than one million pesos (P1,000,000.00) but not more than five million pesos (P5,000,000.00)."

> "Where no prior complaint or investigation has been initiated, any entity that is in doubt as to whether a contemplated act, course of conduct, agreement, or decision, is in compliance with, is exempt from, or is in violation of any of the provisions of this Act, other competition laws, or implementing rules and regulations thereof, may request the Commission, in writing, to render a binding ruling thereon: Provided, That the ruling is for a specified period, subject to extension as may be determined by the Commission, and based on substantial evidence."

> "In the event of an adverse binding ruling on an act, course or conduct, agreement, or decision, the applicant shall be provided with a reasonable period, which in no case shall be more than ninety (90) days, to abide by the ruling of the Commission and shall not be subject to administrative, civil, or criminal action unless the applicant fails to comply with the provisions of this Act;"

> "At any time prior to the conclusion by the Commission of its inquiry, any entity under inquiry may, without in any manner admitting a violation of this Act or any other competition laws, submit to the Commission a written proposal for the entry of a consent order, specifying therein the terms and conditions of the proposed consent order which shall include among others the following: (1) The payment of an amount within the range of fines provided for under this Act; (2) The required compliance report as well as an entity to submit regular compliance reports; (3) Payment of damages to any private party or parties who may have suffered injury; and (4) Other terms and conditions that the Commission deems appropriate and necessary for the effective enforcement of this Act or other Competition Laws: Provided, That a consent order shall not bar any inquiry for the same or similar acts if continued or repeated;"

> "The request for a binding ruling, the show cause order, or the proposal for consent order; the facts, data, and information therein contained or subsequently supplied by the entity or entities concerned; admissions, oral or written, made by them against their interest; all other documents filed by them, including their evidence presented in the proceedings before the Commission; and the judgment or order rendered thereon; shall not be admissible as evidence in any criminal proceedings arising from the same act subject of the binding ruling, show cause order or consent order against such entity or entities, their officers, employees, and agents."

> "Decisions of the Commission shall be appealable to the Court of Appeals in accordance with the Rules of Court. The appeal shall not stay the order, ruling or decision sought to be reviewed, unless the Court of Appeals shall direct otherwise upon such terms and conditions it may deem just. In the appeal, the Commission shall be included as a party respondent to the case."

> "Any person who suffers direct injury by reason of any violation of this Act may institute a separate and independent civil action after the Commission has completed the preliminary inquiry provided under Section 31."

> "Any action arising from a violation of any provision of this Act shall be forever barred unless commenced within five (5) years from: For criminal actions, the time the violation is discovered by the offended party, the authorities, or their agents; and For administrative and civil actions, the time the cause of action accrues."

| Citizenship and character | Every member is a Filipino citizen and resident of good moral character with recognized probity and independence, distinguished in public, civic, or academic service in economics, law, finance, commerce, or engineering |

| Writ of execution | On finality the Commission enforces its own rulings and fines by writ of execution |
| Trial court forum | The Regional Trial Court of the principal place of business takes original and exclusive jurisdiction over every criminal and civil competition case regardless of penalty |
| Private action timing | A directly injured person sues independently only after the Commission completes its preliminary inquiry, so an early filing waits for the administrative gate to open |
| Limitations | Criminal cases prescribe in five years from discovery by the victim, the authorities, or their agents, while administrative and civil cases prescribe in five years from accrual |
| Injunction bar | No court below the Court of Appeals or the Supreme Court may enjoin the Commission, save for extreme constitutional urgency with a bond capped at twenty percent of the Section 29 fines, and a judge who disobeys faces at least one year suspension without pay |
| Transparency | Final decisions with public proceeding records reach the public subject only to the confidentiality rule |

Confidentiality seals filings while consent opens settlement, so secrecy protects candor and candor buys leniency. Advance ruling prevents liability while timely cure defeats it, so doubt favors asking before acting. Administrative inquiry gates private suit while appeal never stays enforcement, so the Commission moves first and the courts review without freezing the market.

### Case: *Gios-Samar v. DOTC*, G.R. No. 217158, March 12, 2019

Facts: A taxpayer group asked the Supreme Court to stop the bundling of six regional airports into two bid bundles and pleaded monopoly, restraint of trade, and dummy arrangements as bare legal conclusions. The petition named no relevant market, no dominant position, and no stockholding facts.

Issue: The issue was whether conclusions of monopoly and restraint suffice without ultimate facts on the relevant market, the dominant position, and the abuse.

Held: The Court dismissed the petition, because monopoly and restraint claims need ultimate facts that only evidence can supply, exclusivity in a concession never equals monopoly by itself, and direct resort to the Court violated the hierarchy of courts where facts still had to be tried.

Doctrine: Conclusions never state a cause of action without ultimate facts. Dominance needs proof of market share with power over price or supply. Abuse needs proof of exclusionary conduct and not of size alone. Remedies begin in the proper forum with facts pleaded, and the Commission is that forum first for competition facts. (Full digest in Part IV-A §3.)

**Q: A business daily demands that the Commission publish the production costs and customer lists a cement maker filed during a cartel inquiry. The maker objects and withholds consent while no court orders disclosure. Must the Commission publish the data.**

A: No. The Commission must deny publication, must keep the production costs and customer lists sealed, and must release only a redacted ruling that protects the sealed data.
Under R.A. No. 10667 Sec. 34 sealing confidential business information submitted in any inquiry or investigation and in any deliberation relating thereto against direct or indirect disclosure, publication, transfer, copying or dissemination, punishable by a fine of not less than P1,000,000.00 but not more than P5,000,000.00, with only three exits of notifying-entity consent, mandatory disclosure by law, or valid order of a court, government or regulatory agency including an exchange, consistent with Gios-Samar v. DOTC (G.R. No. 217158, March 12, 2019) which requires ultimate facts on market, dominance and abuse to be pleaded and proved in the proper forum first rather than aired as conclusions, inquiry filings stay inside the forum until a lawful exit opens.
Here, a business daily demands publication of the production costs and customer lists a cement maker filed during a cartel inquiry, the maker objects and withholds consent while no court orders disclosure, and no mandatory law is invoked, so press demand alone opens none of the three exits and the counter-reading that public interest or press request equals a valid order fails.
The Commission therefore keeps the data sealed, publishes only a ruling redacted to the extent possible, and any official who discloses faces the Sec. 34 fine of P1,000,000.00 to P5,000,000.00.

**Q: A flour distributor directly injured by a price cartel files a civil damages suit in the Regional Trial Court while the Commission preliminary inquiry is still running. The cartel moves to dismiss as premature. Must the court dismiss the suit.**

A: Yes. The court must dismiss the civil damages suit as premature without prejudice, and the flour distributor may refile only after the Commission completes its preliminary inquiry.
Under R.A. No. 10667 Sec. 45 allowing any person who suffers direct injury to institute a separate and independent civil action only after the Commission has completed the preliminary inquiry under Sec. 31, with Sec. 31 giving the Commission sole and exclusive authority to open fact-finding or preliminary inquiry to be completed within ninety days, and Sec. 44 giving the Regional Trial Court original and exclusive jurisdiction over the civil case once the gate opens, as applied in Gios-Samar v. DOTC (G.R. No. 217158, March 12, 2019) which dismissed competition claims pleaded as bare conclusions without ultimate facts on market, dominance and abuse, the inquiry first builds the facts that a court later tries.
Here, a flour distributor directly injured by a price cartel filed civil damages in the Regional Trial Court while the Commission preliminary inquiry is still running, and the cartel moves to dismiss as premature, but direct injury alone does not open the action because completion of the inquiry is a condition precedent, so the early filing jumps the administrative gate and the claim that separateness means immediacy fails.
The court therefore dismisses the suit as premature without prejudice to refiling once the Commission completes its inquiry, leaving the five-year Sec. 46 period from accrual and the later civil jurisdiction intact.

## Exam Traps

1. Attachment never means control, so a presidential directive never binds Commission adjudication.
2. Five members sit but three decide, so two votes never adopt any Commission act.
3. Seven years run without reappointment, so a holdover second term is never allowed.
4. The immediately preceding elections bar candidacy, so a recent losing candidate never qualifies.
5. The next election after cessation bars running, save only for Barangay and Sangguniang Kabataan polls.
6. Two years bar post-service practice before the Commission, and the same bar binds spouses and fourth-degree relatives during incumbency and two years after.
7. Teaching never counts as prohibited practice, while every other professional practice does.
8. Original and primary jurisdiction stays with the Commission on mixed issues, so a sector regulator never ousts it.
9. No other law enforcement agency may run a competition inquiry, save only for deputized aid under Commission direction.
10. Criminal prosecution belongs to the DOJ Office for Competition, so the Commission refers and never prosecutes.
11. First offense caps at P100,000,000.00 while second offense floors at the same figure, so the count changes the range.
12. Daily defiance fines accrue only from day forty-five after receipt, so days one to forty-four never multiply.
13. False information draws up to P1,000,000.00 whether negligent or intentional, so good faith mistake still answers.
14. The fine schedule rises every five years, so a memorized figure silently grows.
15. Divestiture without a failed behavioral alternative is void, so listing among remedies never means free choice.
16. Basic necessities triple every fine, so the commodity traded changes the number.
17. Confidentiality yields only to consent, mandatory law, or valid order, so press demand alone never opens files.
18. Anonymity survives unless expressly waived, so later open testimony without waiver never lifts the shield alone.
19. Binding rulings need no pending case, so an entity already under inquiry never qualifies.
20. Ninety days to obey an adverse ruling is a ceiling and not a floor, so a shorter reasonable period set by the Commission binds.
21. Consent never admits violation and never bars a fresh inquiry on repeated acts, so settlement never buys future immunity.
22. Inquiry papers never enter the criminal case over the same act, so admissions before the Commission never prove the crime.
23. Appeal never stays enforcement unless the Court of Appeals so orders, so losing respondents obey while appealing.
24. Private suit before completed preliminary inquiry is premature, so the victim waits for the administrative gate.
25. Five years run from discovery for crimes but from accrual for civil and administrative cases, so the trigger differs by action.
26. Only the Court of Appeals and the Supreme Court may enjoin the Commission, and urgency needs a constitutional issue with grave irreparable injury plus bond.
