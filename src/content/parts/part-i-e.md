### Case: *Jewel Villacorta v. Insurance Commission and Empire Insurance Co.*, G.R. No. L-54171, Oct. 28, 1980

Facts: Jewel Villacorta owned a 1976 Colt Lancer insured with Empire under Private Car Policy No. MBI/PC-0704 for P35,000.00 own damage, P30,000.00 theft, P30,000.00 third-party liability, May 16, 1977 to May 16, 1978. On May 9, 1978 she left it with Sunday Machine Works for check-up and repairs. On May 11, 1978, while in the shop's custody, six persons took it to Montalban, Rizal, where driver Benito Mabasa crashed into a parked gravel-and-sand truck. Mabasa and one passenger died, four were injured, and the car was extensively damaged. Empire denied the total-loss claim. The Insurance Commission dismissed the complaint, holding the driver was not an authorized driver and the taking was a mere temporary joy ride, hence no theft.

Issue: The first question is whether the shop employee's diversion violated the authorized-driver clause and barred recovery. The second question is whether the unlawful taking was theft within the theft clause.

Held: The petition was granted. The Commission decision was set aside. First, the shop employee was an authorized driver: entrusting the key to an established repair shop presumes permission to drive for legitimate checking or road-testing, and diversion to an illicit purpose does not breach the clause where the driver is duly licensed. Second, independently, the unlawful taking without consent partook of theft under Art. 308 of the Revised Penal Code, so the theft clause applied. Permanent intent to deprive is not required since use itself is gain. Empire was sentenced to pay P35,000.00 with legal interest from filing until full payment plus costs, with subrogation against Sunday Machine Works preserved.

Doctrine: An employee's unauthorized diversion of an entrusted car does not violate the authorized-driver clause so long as he is licensed. An unlawful taking without consent is theft under the theft clause even if temporary. Insurance contracts are contracts of adhesion construed strictly against the insurer to protect the weaker party.

> Exam trap: IC dismissal of a claim is not the end of the road — Sec. 439 gives the adjudicated decision the force of a judgment appealable to the Court of Appeals within thirty (30) days, and Villacorta shows the Court setting aside a restrictive authorized-driver plus no-theft ruling. Plead the thirty-day CA notice, not the Secretary-of-Finance route.

**Q: Villacorta leaves her insured car with a repair shop for check-up and repairs. A shop employee drives it to Montalban without her consent, crashes it into a parked truck, and the car is extensively damaged. The insurer invokes the authorized-driver clause and denies theft, and the Insurance Commission dismisses her complaint on those grounds. Decide whether recovery lies and state the appeal route.**

A: The claim is recoverable under both the authorized-driver and theft covers, so the Commission's dismissal of the own-damage and theft claim must be set aside.
The controlling rules are Sec. 439, which lets the Commissioner adjudicate the insured's own-damage and theft claim up to P5,000,000.00 with appeal by notice to the Court of Appeals within thirty (30) days. The adhesion-construction rule resolves policy ambiguities strictly against the insurer. Art. 308 of the Revised Penal Code treats unlawful taking without consent as theft because use itself is gain.
Applied here, entrusting the key to an established repair shop presumptively authorizes legitimate driving by a licensed driver, so the diversion to an illicit purpose does not breach the clause. The taking without consent is theft regardless of permanence, and here it proved lasting since the car was smashed beyond serviceable return.
Villacorta therefore recovers P35,000.00 with legal interest from filing, subject to the insurer's subrogation against the shop, and the remedy from the adverse ruling is a Sec. 439 notice of appeal to the Court of Appeals filed with the Commissioner within thirty (30) days from receipt.

Historic Bar question: gap. No 2016-2025 Bar question found on certificates of authority, paid-up capital, net worth phase-in, single risk, admitted assets, reserves, investments, examinations, or suspension and liquidation. If examined, anchor on the Sec. 194 phase-in figures and the Sec. 221 twenty-percent rule, and state the Sec. 193 refusal ground in the Commissioner's own judgment language.

**Q: A new domestic non-life insurer with P800,000,000.00 paid-up capital and facially complete papers applies for a certificate of authority, and the Commissioner considers refusing it. Decide whether the certificate may issue and state the refusal ground.**

A: No certificate may issue on these papers, and the Commissioner may additionally refuse any application where refusal best promotes the interest of the people of the country.
The controlling rules are Sec. 194, which requires at least P1,000,000,000.00 in paid-up capital for a new stock life or non-life company, kept unimpaired for continuance. Sec. 193 bars issuance until the Commissioner is satisfied on the four requisites of Philippine-law qualification, local economic justification, sound direction and administration with integrity, and financial organization and capital reasonably assuring the safety of policyholders and the public.
Applied here, P800,000,000.00 falls short of the P1,000,000,000.00 floor regardless of papers. Even at full capital, economic justification, management integrity, and policyholder safety must each still be satisfied.
The application must therefore be denied on the capital floor alone, and refusal is likewise lawful on an adverse best-interest judgment under Sec. 193.

**Q: A non-life insurer controlled by H Corp. proposes to sell to H Corp. an asset worth 6 percent of its admitted assets as of Dec. 31 last year without asking anyone. Applied here, the six-percent asset sale exceeds the five-percent line and falls outside the thirty-day notice band for smaller transactions, while the forty-five-percent stock purchase exceeds the forty-percent presumption and is an acquisition of control by a non-insurer.
Neither transaction may therefore proceed without the required approvals: prior written approval for the sale, and twenty-day notice plus prior written approval for the acquisition.

**Q: The Commissioner places troubled insurer X under conservatorship. X argues the Commissioner should have gone straight to liquidation because its books look insolvent. State the correct sequencing answer with reasons.**

Conservatorship therefore stands, receivership and liquidation follow only as the escalation matures, and a liquidated insurer is never rehabilitated or reauthorized.

## 7. Sales Practices and Consumer Protection

### 7.01 Prohibitions

> (a) No insurance company doing business in the Philippines shall refuse, without just cause, to pay or settle claims arising under coverages provided by its policies, nor shall any such company engage in unfair claim settlement practices. | Sanction | After notice and hearing, each instance is a separate violation and sufficient cause for suspension or revocation of the certificate of authority |

Payment clocks follow. The life-insurance rule comes first:

> The proceeds of a life insurance policy shall be paid immediately upon maturity of the policy, unless such proceeds are made payable in installments or as an annuity, in which case the installments, or annuities shall be paid as they become due: Provided, however, That in the case of a policy maturing by the death of the insured, the proceeds thereof shall be paid within sixty (60) days after presentation of the claim and filing of the proof of death of the insured. Refusal or failure to pay the claim within the time prescribed herein will entitle the beneficiary to collect interest on the proceeds of the policy for the duration of the delay at the rate of twice the ceiling prescribed by the Monetary Board, unless such failure or refusal to pay is based on the ground that the claim is fraudulent. The proceeds of the policy maturing by the death of the insured payable to the beneficiary shall include the discounted value of all premiums paid in advance of their due dates, but are not due and payable at maturity.
>
> — Sec. 248, Insurance Code (RA 10607)

The non-life rule follows:

> The amount of any loss or damage for which an insurer may be liable, under any policy other than life insurance policy, shall be paid within thirty (30) days after proof of loss is received by the insurer and ascertainment of the loss or damage is made either by agreement between the insured and the insurer or by arbitration; but if such ascertainment is not had or made within sixty (60) days after such receipt by the insurer of the proof of loss, then the loss or damage shall be paid within ninety (90) days after such receipt. Refusal or failure to pay the loss or damage within the time prescribed herein will entitle the assured to collect interest on the proceeds of the policy for the duration of the delay at the rate of twice the ceiling prescribed by the Monetary Board, unless such failure or refusal to pay is based on the ground that the claim is fraudulent.
>
> — Sec. 249, Insurance Code (RA 10607)

| Clock | Trigger | Deadline | Delay price |
|---|---|---|---|
| Life, death maturity (Sec. 248) | Presentation of claim plus proof of death | Sixty (60) days | Interest at twice the Monetary Board ceiling, unless the claim is fraudulent |
| Life, otherwise (Sec. 248) | Maturity; installments or annuity as due | Immediately, or as each installment or annuity falls due | Same delay interest rule |
| Non-life, ascertained (Sec. 249) | Receipt of proof of loss plus ascertainment by agreement or arbitration | Thirty (30) days after proof received and ascertained | Same delay interest rule |
| Non-life, unascertained (Sec. 249) | Receipt of proof of loss with no ascertainment within sixty (60) days | Ninety (90) days after receipt of proof | Same delay interest rule |

The litigation consequence is as follows:

> In case of any litigation for the enforcement of any policy or contract of insurance, it shall be the duty of the Commissioner or the Court, as the case may be, to make a finding as to whether the payment of the claim of the insured has been unreasonably denied or withheld; and in the affirmative case, the insurance company shall be adjudged to pay damages which shall consist of attorney's fees and other expenses incurred by the insured person by reason of such unreasonable denial or withholding of payment plus interest of twice the ceiling prescribed by the Monetary Board of the amount of the claim due the insured, from the date following the time prescribed in Section 248 or in Section 249, as the case may be, until the claim is fully satisfied: Provided, That failure to pay any such claim within the time prescribed in said sections shall be considered prima facie evidence of unreasonable delay in payment.
>
> — Sec. 250, Insurance Code (RA 10607)

Fraudulent claims are punished as follows:

> It is unlawful to: (a) Present or cause to be presented any fraudulent claim for the payment of a loss under a contract of insurance; and (b) Fraudulently prepare, make or subscribe any writing with intent to present or use the same, or to allow it to be presented in support of any such claim. Any person who violates this section shall be punished by a fine not exceeding twice the amount claimed or imprisonment of two (2) years, or both, at the discretion of the court.
>
> — Sec. 251, Insurance Code (RA 10607)

| Element/Requisite | Content |
|---|---|
| Sec. 250 finding | Commissioner or Court must rule whether denial or withholding was unreasonable |
| Sec. 250 damages | Attorney's fees plus other expenses caused by the unreasonable denial, plus interest at twice the Monetary Board ceiling from the day after the Sec. 248 or 249 deadline until full satisfaction |
| Sec. 250 presumption | Non-payment within the Sec. 248 or 249 period is prima facie evidence of unreasonable delay |
| Sec. 251 acts | Presenting or causing presentation of a fraudulent claim; fraudulently preparing, making, or subscribing a writing to support one |
| Sec. 251 penalty | Fine not exceeding twice the amount claimed, or imprisonment of two (2) years, or both, at the court's discretion |

The rebate, misrepresentation, and discrimination bans read as follows:

> No insurance company doing business in the Philippines or any agent thereof, no insurance broker, and no employee or other representative of any such insurance company, agent, or broker, shall make, procure or negotiate any contract of insurance or agreement as to policy contract, other than is plainly expressed in the policy or other written contract issued or to be issued as evidence thereof, or shall directly or indirectly, by giving or sharing a commission or in any manner whatsoever, pay or allow or offer to pay or allow to the insured or to any employee of such insured, either as an inducement to the making of such insurance or after such insurance has been effected, any rebate from the premium which is specified in the policy, or any special favor or advantage in the dividends or other benefits to accrue thereon, or shall give or offer to give any valuable consideration or inducement of any kind, directly or indirectly, which is not specified in such policy or contract of insurance; nor shall any such company, or any agent thereof, as to any policy or contract of insurance issued, make any discrimination against any Filipino in the sense that he is given less advantageous rates, dividends or other policy conditions or privileges than are accorded to other nationals because of his race.
>
> — Sec. 370, Insurance Code (RA 10607)

> No insurance company doing business in the Philippines, and no officer, director, or agent thereof, and no insurance broker or any other person, partnership or corporation shall issue or circulate or cause or permit to be issued or circulated any literature, illustration, circular or statement of any sort misrepresenting the terms of any policy issued by any insurance company of the benefits or advantages promised thereby, or any misleading estimate of the dividends or share of surplus to be received thereon, or shall use any name or title of any policy or class of policies misrepresenting the true nature thereof; nor shall any such company or agent thereof, or any other person, partnership or corporation make any misleading representation or incomplete comparison of policies to any person insured in such company for the purpose of inducing or tending to induce such person to lapse, forfeit, or surrender his said insurance.
>
> — Sec. 371, Insurance Code (RA 10607)

> If the Commissioner, after notice and hearing, finds that any insurance company, rating organization, agent, broker or other person has violated any of the provisions of this title, it shall order the payment of a fine not to exceed Twenty-five thousand pesos (P25,000.00) for each such offense, and shall immediately suspend or revoke the license issued to such insurance company, rating organization, agent, or broker. The issuance, procurement or negotiation of a single policy or contract of insurance shall be deemed a separate offense.
>
> — Sec. 372, Insurance Code (RA 10607)

## Exam Traps

1. Ordinary Commissioner decisions go to the Secretary of Finance. Only adjudicated Sec. 439 claims go to the Court of Appeals — and the CA notice runs thirty (30) days from receipt, filed with the Commissioner.
2. The Sec. 439 ceiling is P5,000,000.00 per single claim excluding interest, cost, and attorney's fees. Company-vs-agent disputes are outside it entirely.
3. P800,000,000.00 paid-up fails the P1,000,000,000.00 new-company floor regardless of papers — and even full capital still needs the four Sec. 193 satisfaction requisites.
4. The phase-in build is cumulative on fixed dates (2013/2016/2019/2022). State all four dates, not just the current total.
5. Net-worth deficiency triggers a fifteen (15)-day pro-rata cash makeup with no new risk meanwhile — the paying stockholder gets a lien, not a windfall.
6. Single-risk retention is twenty percent (20%) of net worth per subject of insurance, non-life only — deduct authorized reinsurance ceded (plus co-surety and pledged security for surety) before testing.
7. Foreign deposit is at least P1,000,000,000.00 with fifty percent (50%) Government debt — a Philippine-net-worth concept, releasable only on written approval after zero-liability proof.
8. Suspension needs the Sec. 254 opinion triggers. Restoration needs a three (3)-year business plan — conservation is the next rung, not the same act.
9. Conservator overrules prior management and board, reports to the Commissioner, and exits either to restored operation or to Title 15 — never sideways to private settlement.
10. Receivership starts with forthwith cease-and-desist. The ninety (90)-day reorganization determination is mandatory before liquidation. Liquidation needs insolvency-plus-public-interest.
11. Insolvency means failure to maintain Sec. 200 solvency requirements — inability to pay lawful obligations as they fall due — not balance-sheet opinion.
12. Commissioner distress actions are final and executory. Courts intervene only on convincing proof of plain arbitrariness and bad faith, with bond mechanics and Rule 58.
13. A liquidated insurer is never rehabilitated or reauthorized — the one absolute end state in the chapter.
14. Control is presumed at forty percent (40%). Five percent (5%) deals need prior approval while the notice band runs over one-half percent (1/2%) to under five percent (5%) — classify before answering.
15. Acquiring control needs twenty (20) days notice plus prior approval. The Commissioner disapproves only on the stated Sec. 302 factors.
16. Agent serves the company, broker serves the insured, adjuster serves one side per license type — and an agent is an independent contractor, not an employee (Sec. 309).
17. Below-file pricing violates Sec. 365 even with client consent. Above-file pricing needs Sec. 366 written approval first. Off-policy value to the insured violates Sec. 370 always.
18. The five Sec. 247 practices each need without-just-cause plus business-practice frequency — isolated delay is a clock problem (Secs. 248-250), not yet a suspension ground.
19. Missed payment clocks price at twice the Monetary Board ceiling automatically. Unreasonableness adds attorney's fees — and missing the clock is prima facie unreasonable.
20. Each Sec. 372 policy is a separate offense to P25,000.00 with suspension or revocation — count policies, not schemes.
21. Prior approval (Sec. 232) gates every form, application, rider, clause, warranty, and endorsement — Sec. 231 is the annual-statement publication rule, not the gate.
22. Insurers and pre-need companies are covered persons (Sec. 3(a)(2)). Knowing non-reporting is itself money laundering. Only the Supreme Court enjoins a CA freeze.
